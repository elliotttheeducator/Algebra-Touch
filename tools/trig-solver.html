Trigonometry — SOH CAH TOA
  1. Spot the hypotenuse first — it's always the longest side, directly opposite the right angle, and it never touches the right angle.
  2. Label Opposite and Adjacent relative to the angle you're given — Opposite is across from it, Adjacent is the other side touching it.
  3. Match your two sides to a ratio — Sin needs Opposite & Hypotenuse, Cos needs Adjacent & Hypotenuse, Tan needs Opposite & Adjacent.
  4. Write the equation, then rearrange it so the unknown is by itself.
  5. Check your calculator is in degree mode, not radians — it's the single most common mistake here.
Pythagoras' Theorem
  1. c² = a² + b² — c is always the hypotenuse (the longest side, opposite the right angle); a and b are the two shorter legs.
  2. Missing the hypotenuse? Square both legs, add them, then square-root the total.
  3. Missing a leg? Rearrange first — a² = c² − b² — square the hypotenuse, subtract the known leg squared, then square-root what's left.
  4. Square first, combine second, root last — doing it out of order is the most common slip.
